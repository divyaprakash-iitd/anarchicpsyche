Many of us had returned back to Gopeshwar after visiting our home towns during the summer vacation of our institute. We were desperately looking forward to an outing. The plan was to go on a casual hike, or so we thought. On the eve of our planned hike ominous clouds had started appearing in the sky. We hadn't had a proper visit to the mountains in a couple of months thus the prospect of rains dampened our spirits.

![](img_20180227_180640-01.jpeg)

Hiking during rainy weather in the mountains is a dangerous affair due to occurrences of flash floods, landslides, thunder strikes (rare but still a possibility in high up places). The last few days had been dry and thus it irritated us to think that the clouds had chosen to shed their weight now. With nothing else to do but hope we signed off for the night, woken up a few times by the ear-splitting sound of thunder (it gets really loud in the mountains). Unknown to us, rains of this night were busy transforming the rivers and creeks of Mandal into a sight to behold.

![](img_20180104_065829.jpg)

We were delighted the next morning to see clear skies all around. We thanked our good fortune and got ready quite quickly so that we could start as early as possible. Our hiking party consisted of 5 people, including me and Abhigna. Three of our friends were to accompany us, Lijo, Lijin and Avinash. Their homes were at an elevation of about 400 m below ours, closer to the Alaknanda river. They showed up on time as planned on a hired vehicle and picked us up. We all proceeded towards Mandal village, the place from where the hike to Anusuya Devi Temple starts.

![](img_20191008_113405.jpg)

I was fortunate to capture the entire trek route, from Mandal village to the temple, while coming down from Rudranath in October 2019. Apart from the places in this blog, one can also see [Chandrashila peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrashila) (4000 m above sea level), home of the highest Shiva temple in the world, Tunganath. I have some wonderful memories of these places too which I'll share in my upcoming posts.

We were dropped off at the gates of Ma Anusuya Devi Temple. It's right on the roadside. However the actual temple is quite a hike away. Our driver, Govind Bhai, our go-to guy for any vehicle requirements, told us to be back soon. Taking our non-pahadiness into account, he expected us to be back at around 3-4 PM. We walked a few minutes down a road and wondered why didn't our driver just continue on it.

![](img_20180714_071149.jpg)

However only a few meters ahead, the road crossed a river on a bridge and ended abruptly on the other side. Amused by the strange sight, I turned to my friends to inquire what they thought of it and found all of them staring not at the bridge but its background.

![](img_20180714_071323-effects.jpg)

Innumerable waterfalls were reflecting the morning sun along the side of the mountain. This could have been only possible because of the heavy rains last night. The rains had emptied the clouds reducing them to wisps, leaving the sky a shade of blue which could only have come from a painter's palette. All the rain water was now flowing along the sides of the mountain atop which the temple stood. We started counting the number of waterfalls but soon gave up realizing the futility of our efforts. Not only was it pointless to count them, in the process we were missing the precious moments of this wonderful scene conjured up for us by the rain gods. We happily just admired them for a while and it was quite some time before any of us started moving again.

![](img_20180714_073619_hdr1.jpg)

We got on the path, and went through the village. The old style pahadi homes are really small, even though they are two-storied, probably to keep the warmth inside. We came across a mini waterfall on the way and it seemed. We got so excited that we spent quite some time adoring it and clicking pictures around it. We had no idea that this did not represent even a small percentage of what was in store for us.

![](img_20180714_074225.jpg)

We continued on the path, being led by a buffalo for some time, she seemed to wait for us if we stopped. Or perhaps she was wondering why we were following her.

![](img_20180714_074957-1.jpg)

We kept walking ahead, always under the shadow of the sheer wall in front of us, atop which lay our destination. Strangely it seemed intimidating and awe-inspiring simultaneously.

![](img_20180714_074911-1.jpg)

We walked on the path with a jungle on one side and the rapidly flowing river on the other. At many places, waterfalls from our left side fell down from the forests and went under and across our path to join the river on our right.

![](img_20180714_091149-01.jpeg)

Every now and then we paused and looked at each other conveying the blissful state of our minds through our gaze. Every one of us seemed genuinely happy and it showed in the springiness of our steps along the hilly gradients.

![](img_20180714_091822-01-1.jpeg)

A kilometer or so further down the route, a small path descended sideways. This was the opportunity that we had been heartily seeking. Without any hesitation we all descended down the path and came to the rocky banks of the river.

![](img_20180714_085009_hdr-01.jpeg "Credits: https://www.instagram.com/trekncook/?hl=en")

The river was split into two parts by a pile of boulders. The roughly flowing rapids went one way, away from us, while a calm, narrow stream gently flowed towards us. We all went down to this stream and dipped our feet in its cold and fresh waters.

![](img_20180714_084021_hdr-01.jpeg "Credits: https://www.instagram.com/trekncook/?hl=en")

Me and Lijo climbed up a few boulders and took pictures of the scary part of the river. The other bank of the river was held in place by a steep, almost vertical mountain side. Many waterfalls adorned this rocky wall and joined the river.

![](output.gif)

We all sat down each occupying a personal boulder and lost ourselves in the calmness of the noisy river. I can't recall a moment in my life when I have been this close to my spiritual self and have felt such happiness.

![](img_20180714_085033_hdr.jpg "This is my Happy face! Pure and unadulterated happiness!")

My mind completely satiated after feasting on nature's visual and aural platter, it was time to listen to my envious and empty stomach's growls. It had been quite some time since we last had anything to eat. We walked once again, keeping an eye out for any place offering food to the pilgrims. We didn't have to walk long. There was a hut just after crossing the river which had food as well as lodging facilities (a cozy bed and some blankets).

![](img_20180714_093957_hdr.jpg)

We ordered the staple mountain food for hikers, a hot plate of Maggi, along with chai (obviously!). We walked about the bridge, taking some pictures and talking with the shopkeepers. The sun was now shining directly on us, making us seek shade under the hut's canopy. The day had started to become humid and that meant that rains would probably greet us in the latter part of the day.

![](img_20180714_092104-01.jpeg)

Sensing the upcoming rainy weather, we quickly gobbled up our breakfast and got back on the ever upward sloping path on this side of the bridge. We even came across a few leeches on the path, and did our best to stay clear of them. Many a times we would hear the sound of streams and water falling just beyond the canopy of trees. However, we had to resist our temptation to go looking for them as we were now getting a bit late and we also had the prospects of rains to worry about.

![](img_20180714_102341_hdr.jpg)

Along the way we came across native women carrying huge stacks of grass on their backs. The mountain ladies are very strong and do most of the physically demanding household work. In Gopeshwar I had seen women carrying up to two gas cylinders on their shoulders and walking up on a steep road, whereas I struggled to carry an empty cylinder up the steps of my home. Impressed and admiring the grit of these women, we continued forward.

![](whatsapp-image-2020-08-29-at-4.47.54-pm.jpeg "Credits: https://www.instagram.com/trekncook/?hl=en")

The weather changed, as predicted, for the rest of the journey. Heavy rains ensued and forced us to take shelter. The rains didn't last long though and soon we were back on track. I had my first encounter with a leech in my life. Fortunately I pried it off me, before it could take a proper hold. We stopped momentarily at many places and took many pictures mentally and with our cameras. After about two hours of continuous hiking since breakfast, we finally reached the first part of our destination.

![](img_20180714_123307_hdr.jpg)

Being the only ones at the temple, we were welcomed and greeted by the priest. We prayed for a while inside the temple. It seemed we would be the last ones to arrive for the day, as the priest closed the temple doors soon after we were done praying. We went outside and started walking around the courtyard. Mountains and clouds surrounded us in all directions.

![](img_20180714_122754_hdr.jpg)

I looked up at the sky and wondered which obscured mountains lay beyond the clouds. I was unaware that my future self was looking back at me from one of those mountain tops, through his camera lens.

![](img_9145-01.jpeg "Picture taken in October 2019, from Pitradhar (Elevation: 4100 m), on the [Rudranath trek](../../../../2021/07/16/german-se-ladai-aur-rudranath-ki-chadhai/).")

The priest looked over at us and seemed to discuss something with his colleague. He then came over and asked us if we were going further ahead on the route towards Atri Muni Ashram. We replied in the affirmative. He then hopefully asked us if we could do a small favor for him, to which we readily agreed. He then handed me a bag full of Tulasi leaves and told us that this has been requested by a sage living in the forest along the way and we are supposed to make the delivery. He gave a brief description of his hut so that we could spot it from the path.

After a brief visit to the [Lord Dattatreya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dattatreya), Anusuya Devi's son in a nearby temple, we continued the journey towards his father Atri Muni's Ashram. Although the priest at the temple had warned us about the paths being flooded due to the recent rains, we wanted to see it for ourselves. More importantly, we now also had a delivery job to complete.

![](img_20180714_131525_hdr.jpg)

We walked along always keeping an eye out for any match to the description of the sage's hut given to us. The hut supposedly had a blue tarp placed on its roof, to protect it from the rains. We spotted it after a while down the slope on the right side of the road. Eager to meet the sage, we all excitedly went down the wet pathway strewn with leaves and the occasional leech trying with all its might to attach itself to some living creature.

![](img_20180714_131024_hdr.jpg)

"How many of you are there?"  
He peeked outside, did a mental head count and seemed hopeful. "Would you guys care to join me for a task that's been pending for some time?"  
Why not? We thought. How often do you get to help an ascetic in a jungle? We all agreed although I couldn't help wondering what the task actually was. We were withheld this information and just asked to follow him down a slope. He said he'll just show us instead of explaining. He thanked us for the Tulasi leaves and kept the bag inside his hut. He then put on his sandals and led us deeper into the forest. After walking for a few minutes through the forest we started to hear a familiar sound and it seemed that we would soon be approaching another waterfall. I wondered what task the sage had in his mind. I hoped that he had not overestimated our capabilities. The lush green and wet forest ended and we emerged at a set of stairs leading down to the source of a heart penetrating sound.

![](return_output.gif)

In front of us mother nature had laid out a scene much sought after by cameramen of those "Planet Earth" documentaries. A rapid, untamed river flowed down the mountain on our left and then past a makeshift wooden log bridge on our right, and fell into unknown depths again, the cliffs reverberating its sound in the forest.

![](output-1.gif)

For the umpteenth time, it again seemed that the beauty of the scene we were witnessing couldn't be surpassed. But we had been proven wrong on all the earlier occasions and none of us were complaining. After cherishing the scene to our full satisfaction we looked at our guide. He wasn't in the lead anymore. He had gone up a path on our left, towards a gated cave, which seemed to be the manifestation of a mountain lover's dream home.

![](img_20180714_142025-01-1.jpeg)

It turned out to be his home during the summer months. The place became risky to stay during monsoons because of the fear of flash floods. He soon came out of the cave-home with some tools and led us to the bridge. It was there that he briefed us on our mission.

![](img_20180714_134139_hdr-01.jpeg)

A driftwood which was basically a large tree root was stuck under the bridge. This gave leverage to the fast flowing water behind it and the bridge was in danger of being pushed from its anchor points, down the river and into the waterfall up ahead. His plan was to use a log or some tool as a lever and try to lift the bridge from one side above its anchor point. This should provide enough space for the driftwood to flow underneath the bridge and solve our problems.

![](output-2.gif)

There was another small cave nearby, in which wooden logs were stored. He directed us to take one from there and use it as a lever to lift the bridge. After going through some of the inventory, we decided on a log and carried it to the bridge.

![](img_20180714_134206_hdr-01.jpeg)

He placed a large rock near the bridge to act as a fulcrum. We put one end of the log into a crevice under the bridge and over the rock and pushed with all our might down the other end. The log snapped! We should have expected that. The bridge was bulky. We were then handed an iron rod, supplied from the cave, and got back to work.

![](output-3.gif)

Eventually our effort paid off and the bridge moved just enough for the stuck root to flow under it. Filled with a sense of accomplishment, we all celebrated. The sage told us that the bridge had been built by him along with the help of the local villagers and thus it was very dear to him. Very pleased with us, he invited us for a cup of tea in his home. We all happily obliged.

All that time spent in strenuous work justified some moments to be spent in vanity and thus we took turns posing next to our accomplished work.

![](img_20180714_141356_hdr-01.jpeg)

We spent a few more moments relaxing next to the river. The way forward to Atri Muni's Ashram was blocked due to the overflowing streams and thus we were advised not to go ahead. We all were too exhausted anyway, thus we decided to come back again some other day. Before leaving, we all posed for a selfie.

![](37133923_1669343949780855_4421369295917809664_o.jpg)

The sage laid out mats for us inside his hut and took out a tetra pack of milk and some biscuits. He explained that the nearby villagers and pilgrims on this route regularly supply his ration out of devotion and respect. It must really take some guts to live this deep in the jungles, year after year. While the water boiled, we all introduced ourselves to babaji. As for his introduction, it surprised us when he revealed that he was an accomplished eye surgeon. However, he had left it all behind him and was living blissfully in the lap of mother nature for the past many years.

![](img_20180714_151239.jpg)

The tea was ready and he offered it to all of us along with a platter of biscuits. We had a long discussion with him on arbitrary topics and in the process gained quite some wisdom, however you'll have to climb up the mountain yourself if you want to acquire it. (Actually, I am saving that for another post. :] ). Soon it was time to leave. He thanked us for all our help, and made one last request. He had a few solar panels to take care of his lighting needs at night. However the inverter battery had run out of electrolyte. He asked us to inform a shopkeeper in Gopeshwar about this. The shopkeeper was a regular on this route and would deliver the battery-water to him on his next trip. We finally bid goodbye and left for Mandal.

![](img_20180714_171004.jpg)

On our way back Abhigna slipped on the wet, stone path. It seemed that she might have sprained her ankle which was worrisome because we had almost the entire route left to cover. However the fall turned out to be a blessing in disguise. In order to check the damage, she removed her sandals and shockingly found out that two leeches had been happily sucking blood on her foot for the past who knows how many hours. We had encountered some leeches on the floor in babaji's hut and he had sprinkled salt on them to keep them at bay. We didn't expect babaji's wisdom to come to our help so soon. We looked around and saw a shop next to the temple. One of us quickly went and brought some salt and sprayed it on Abhigna's feet. The leeches soon loosened their grip and we were finally able to get rid of them. Checking all our shoes, sandals, shirts and pants thoroughly for leeches we again descended down the pathway.

![](img_20180714_181633.jpg)

Our driver was worried sick, since it was almost 6 PM when we finally reached his vehicle. He had asked people going up the mountain to look for us and had given them our description. He feared that we had been washed away in one of the overflowing streams like some tourists a few years ago. As compensation (maybe not according to him) we gave him a detailed account of all the adventures we had been a part of on our way back home.

![](img_20180714_185655.jpg "Heading home towards Gopeshwar.")
